## The difference between urgent and non-urgent repairs

A repair is non-urgent if the renter can continue to safely live in the property. Examples of non-urgent repairs include a broken dishwasher or loose bathroom tiles. Anything not on the urgent repairs list is a non-urgent repair.

## List of urgent repairs

Urgent repairs must be done immediately. The legally defined urgent repairs are: a burst water service; a blocked or broken toilet system; a serious roof leak; a gas leak; a dangerous electrical fault; flooding or serious flood damage; serious storm or fire damage; a failure or breakdown of an essential service or appliance for hot water, water, cooking, heating or laundering; a failure or breakdown of the gas, electricity or water supply; a failure or breakdown of a cooling appliance or service provided by the rental provider; the property not meeting the rental minimum standards; a safety-related device (such as a smoke alarm or pool fence) not working; an appliance, fitting or fixture that is broken and causing a lot of water to be wasted; a building fault that causes pests, mould or damp; and a serious problem with a lift or staircase.

## Organising and paying for repairs

Rental providers must organise and pay for all repairs if the damage is not the renter's fault. Renters should tell the rental provider about repairs in writing as soon as possible. For urgent repairs, renters must contact the rental provider immediately, using the emergency phone number provided at the start of the agreement, and confirm the request in writing.

## Renter options for urgent repairs

If the rental provider doesn't respond to an urgent repair request, the renter can organise and pay for the repair themselves if it costs no more than $2,500. The rental provider must pay the renter back within 7 days. For urgent repairs costing more than $2,500, renters should apply to Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria (RDRV).

## Non-urgent repairs

Written repair requests should include the date. Rental providers must complete non-urgent repairs within 14 days of the date of the written request.

## Who can make a repair

Work that requires a licence must be done by a qualified professional. All repairs must be made to the standard a tradesperson would make them, whether they are done by the renter or the rental provider.

## Paying rent during repairs

Renters must continue paying rent while waiting for repairs. However, they can apply to RDRV to have their rent paid into a special account held by Consumer Affairs Victoria (the Rent Special Account) until the matter is resolved.

## When the rental provider does not respond

Renters should keep records of their attempts to contact the rental provider. If a non-urgent repair has not been done within 14 days, renters can request a free repairs inspection from Consumer Affairs Victoria, which can direct the rental provider to complete the work.

## Rules when the renter is at fault

If the renter caused the damage, the rental provider must give a written repair notice specifying the damage and whether the renter should repair it or the rental provider will repair it at the renter's expense. If the renter organises the repair, the work must be completed within 14 days to a tradesperson's standard.

## Efficient replacements

When replacing water, gas or electrical appliances, rental providers must choose items that meet minimum efficiency standards.

## Resolving repair disputes

Renters can request a free repairs inspection when there is a dispute about who caused the damage for a non-urgent repair, or when the rental provider ignores repair requests. RDRV is a free alternative dispute resolution service that handles repair disputes, by phone, online or in person at VCAT offices. If RDRV doesn't resolve the dispute, renters can apply to VCAT for a compensation order or repair order.

## Entry for repairs

Rental providers must give at least 24 hours notice to enter the property for repairs, and can only enter between 8am and 6pm on a day that is not a public holiday. Urgent repairs may be an exception if the renter agrees.
