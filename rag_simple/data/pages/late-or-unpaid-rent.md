## Informing a rental provider of late rent

If a renter is late paying their rent because they haven't paid by the due date, or has not paid at all, they are 'in arrears'. As a renter, you are responsible for paying your rent on time and as agreed in the rental agreement. If you know your rent is going to be late, try to speak to your rental provider as soon as possible and try to negotiate a new time to pay. Rental providers don't have to be flexible about payment amounts or dates and can take formal action against a renter who is late paying rent. However, it is illegal for a rental provider or agent to charge any sort of 'administration fee' or 'late fee' to process rent payments even if the rent is late.

## Rental provider can issue a notice to vacate for unpaid rent

If renters are more than 14 days late on rent (7 days for rooming house or caravan park residents), the rental provider can give them a notice to vacate. A notice to vacate must be given by hand, post or electronic communication (such as email); electronic communication can only be used if the renter has consented to receiving notices that way. A renter can challenge a notice to vacate by applying to VCAT. A rental provider, rooming house operator or park owner cannot personally evict a renter, physically or otherwise.

## Possession orders and payment plans

If a renter doesn't pay the late rent by the date in the notice to vacate, the rental provider can apply to VCAT for a possession order (an 'eviction notice'), which can be enforced by police. VCAT can issue the possession order or allow the renter (in special circumstances) to pay via a payment plan. If the parties agree on a payment plan and the renter pays as agreed, the possession order application will be dismissed and the rental agreement will continue. If the renter does not pay, the hearing can be fast-tracked and VCAT can issue the possession order.

## Limits on how often a renter can pay late

When a renter is given a notice to vacate because they have not paid rent, they can pay the rent owing by the vacate date stated on the notice to avoid eviction. Even if the matter goes to VCAT, if the amount is paid by the vacate date, the notice no longer applies. However, if more than four notices to vacate for unpaid rent are issued to a renter in a 12-month period, the renter has '4 strikes' against them; the strikes are cleared at the end of the 12 months if there are no more notices. If a fifth notice to vacate is given for unpaid rent, the rental provider can apply to VCAT for a possession order and VCAT can't dismiss the application solely because the renter could pay off the unpaid rent under a payment plan.

## Unpaid rent when the renter has died

When a sole renter dies, their next of kin or legal representative can deal with the rental provider to end the rental agreement. Rent doesn't need to be paid after the property has been vacated, even if it is before the end of the agreement. When a co-renter dies, the other renters are responsible for the rent.
