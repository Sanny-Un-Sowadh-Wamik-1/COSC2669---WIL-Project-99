## What is a notice to vacate?

When a rental provider (landlord) wants a renter to move out, they can talk to the renter to reach an agreement or give the renter a notice to vacate. A notice to vacate is a formal statement that the rental provider wants to end the rental agreement, and can only be given for certain reasons. If a renter does not leave by the termination date in the notice, the rental provider can apply to VCAT for a possession order.

## Reasons rental providers can and cannot give notice

Rental providers can only give notice for the reasons listed below; other reasons are not valid. A rental provider must give a reason and cannot give notice without a valid reason. A rental provider cannot give someone notice for doing something, or saying they will do something, they are legally allowed to do: for example, a rental provider cannot give a renter a notice to vacate for requesting repairs, asking to have a pet, or challenging a rent increase. A notice to vacate is not valid if it is unlawful discrimination. A rental provider may ask a renter to leave without giving a notice to vacate; the renter does not have to agree.

## Immediate notice

A rental provider can give a notice to vacate immediately if the renter or their visitor intentionally or recklessly causes serious damage to the property, including safety equipment and common areas, or puts neighbours, the rental provider, the agent, or their contractors or employees in danger.

## How to give a notice to vacate

Rental providers should use the Notice to vacate to renter/s of rented premises form. The notice must be addressed to the renter, give the reason for ending the agreement, be signed by the rental provider or agent, be sent with enough time to reach the renter, and state the termination date. It must be delivered by registered post, by hand, or by electronic communication (such as email) if the renter has consented to receive notices that way.

## Challenging a notice to vacate

A renter can challenge a notice to vacate if they believe it was not given properly, disagree with the reason given, or were given the notice as a result of family violence. If a renter does not leave after being given notice, the rental provider can apply for a possession order.

## Ending an agreement early: reasons and notice periods

A rental provider can only end a rental agreement before its end date for these reasons (minimum notice in brackets): the renter or visitor intentionally or recklessly causes serious damage (immediate); the renter or visitor puts others in danger (immediate); the premises are unfit for human habitation or destroyed (immediate); the renter or anyone living there seriously threatens or intimidates the rental provider, agent, contractors or employees (14 days); the renter owes at least 14 days rent (14 days); the renter has failed to comply with a VCAT compliance order (14 days); the renter has already been given 2 breach of duty notices and the same breach occurs again (14 days); the property is used for illegal purposes (14 days); the renter has brought in other renters or sub-letters without consent (14 days); the renter has not paid the bond as agreed (14 days); a child under 16 lives there when the agreement says this is not allowed (14 days); the renter misled a government housing authority to get social housing (14 days); drug-related activity in public housing (14 days); the renter is keeping a pet without consent and VCAT has made an order excluding the pet (28 days).

## Notice to vacate at the end of a fixed term or during a periodic agreement

Rental providers cannot issue a notice to vacate at the end of a fixed term agreement without a valid reason. Generally, the standard notice for a valid reason is 90 days, but in some situations it can be less. For a fixed-term agreement, the termination date must be on or after the end date of the agreement. For a periodic (month-by-month) agreement, a notice to vacate can be issued at any time, provided it is for a valid reason. Some reasons require evidence to be supplied with the notice; if required evidence is not supplied, the notice is invalid.

## Valid reasons, evidence and notice periods

The rental provider plans to move in at the end of the fixed term, where this was listed in the additional terms of the agreement (section 91ZW): evidence is the rental agreement and a witnessed statutory declaration by each rental provider; 14 days notice. Reconstruction, repairs or renovations that cannot go ahead unless the renter leaves: evidence is photos and a tradesperson's contract or quote, or a building permit (only a building permit is sufficient for renovations); 90 days notice (previously 60 days). Demolition: building permit and contract with a builder-demolisher; 90 days notice; the premises must not be re-let as a residence for 6 months unless VCAT approves. Use for another purpose such as a business: statutory declaration plus ABN, business registration or planning permit; 90 days notice. The rental provider, their immediate family (including parents and parents-in-law) or a dependant will move in: witnessed statutory declaration naming the person; 90 days notice; the premises must not be re-let to anyone else for 6 months unless VCAT approves. The property is to be sold or put up for sale and vacated at the end of the agreement: contract of sale, agent's authority to sell, or a contract prepared by a conveyancer or lawyer; 90 days notice; the rental provider cannot shorten the agreement for this reason and must not re-let the premises as a residence for 6 months unless VCAT approves. A government authority needs the property for public purposes: acquisition details or letter; 90 days notice. The renter no longer meets social housing eligibility criteria: 90 days notice.

## Mortgagee and deceased renter

A mortgagee (lender) repossessing the property can give a renter a notice to vacate under specific provisions. If a sole renter dies, the rental provider can end the agreement by giving a notice to vacate to the renter's legal personal representative or next of kin.
