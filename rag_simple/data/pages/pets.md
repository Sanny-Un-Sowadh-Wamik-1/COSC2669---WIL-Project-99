## Overview

Renters who want to have a pet in the property must ask their rental provider (landlord) for permission. Rental providers must have a good reason to refuse the renter's request, and can apply to VCAT for an order to refuse permission. All renters, including those with pets, must keep the property clean, avoid damage and not be a nuisance to others. A pet means any animal except an assistance dog, which is a dog trained to help a person with a disability. Assistance dogs do not count as pets, and people with assistance dogs cannot be discriminated against.

## Pet requests

Renters who want to keep a pet should complete a Pet request form (a separate form for each pet), give it to the rental provider and keep a copy. The rental provider has 14 days (starting the day after they receive the form) to make a decision. If they agree, they should give consent in writing. If they don't agree, they must apply to VCAT and tell the renter they have done so within the 14 days; the renter can't keep the pet while VCAT is making a decision. If the rental provider does not respond at all within the 14 days, the renter can keep the pet. Renters should also check that the pet complies with local council laws, which apply whether or not the rental provider has given consent.

## Negotiating conditions for keeping a pet

The rental provider can try to negotiate conditions for keeping a pet, for example that the pet is not allowed inside. If the renter does not agree to the conditions and the rental provider wants to exclude the pet, they must apply to VCAT. Any agreed conditions should be put in writing.

## VCAT's role

If the rental provider applies to VCAT, VCAT can order that the refusal is reasonable and/or the pet is excluded, or that the renter can keep the pet. VCAT may consider the type of pet, the type of property, the appliances, fixtures and fittings, other relevant laws such as council laws, and anything else it considers relevant. If VCAT makes an order excluding the pet, the renter has 14 days to comply. If the renter has not complied after that, the rental provider can give them a notice to vacate with a minimum of 28 days' notice.

## Pets causing damage

If a renter does not meet their duties (for example a pet causes damage), the rental provider can give them a Notice of breach of duty. If the renter does not comply, the rental provider can apply to VCAT for a compensation or compliance order. VCAT will consider fair wear and tear and the age and condition of the damaged items. If a rental provider reasonably believes a renter is keeping a pet without consent, they can apply to VCAT for an order to exclude the pet.

## Pet bonds

Rental providers and owners cannot ask for an additional bond as a 'pet bond'.

## Pets in apartments and units

If the property is an apartment or unit, the owners corporation may have its own rules about pets in common areas, which the renter must follow. If the owners corporation decides the pet is dangerous or is causing a nuisance, the renter may have to remove the pet.
