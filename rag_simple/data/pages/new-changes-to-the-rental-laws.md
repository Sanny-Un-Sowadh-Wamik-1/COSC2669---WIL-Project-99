## Overview

New rules are making renting fairer and more secure for Victorians. If you're renting, you'll have stronger rights and protections, greater security over your lease and finances and improved safety in your home. If you're a rental provider, you'll have clearer obligations and better processes for managing your property.

## Upcoming changes from 13 October 2026

Strengthened bond claims: rental providers must notify the renter in advance if they will be making a claim on the bond at the end of the rental agreement and must provide evidence to support the claim.

Records showing compliance with rental minimum standards: documentation demonstrating property compliance must be retained and provided to Consumer Affairs Victoria upon request.

Mandatory gas and electrical safety checks every 2 years: rental providers and their agents must arrange gas and electrical safety checks by a qualified tradesperson every two years for all rental agreements.

Gas check before draughtproofing: a licensed gasfitter inspection is required within 6 months prior to draughtproofing work.

Ban on rental provider and agent application fees: it will be an offence for the rental provider or agent to charge a renter fees to make a rental application.

## Upcoming changes phased from 1 March 2027

New minimum energy efficiency standards: properties must meet standards covering heating, cooling, hot water systems, showerheads, ceiling insulation and draughtproofing, with phased implementation.

## Changes in effect from 25 November 2025

Ban on no fault evictions: rental providers cannot issue a renter with a notice to vacate without a valid reason, even at the end of a fixed-term agreement. Fixed-term agreements automatically convert to periodic agreements when they expire unless a new agreement is made.

Extended notice periods: rental providers must give a renter 90 days' notice of a rent increase and certain notices to vacate.

Ban on all types of rental bidding: agents and rental providers are banned from accepting offers made by a renter to pay rent higher than the advertised amount or to pay more than one month's rent in advance.

Minimum standards at advertising: it is an offence for a rental provider or their agent to advertise a property that does not meet the minimum standards.

Mandatory annual smoke alarm checks: rental providers and their agents must arrange annual smoke alarm safety checks for all rental agreements.

Protection of renters' personal information: it is an offence to disclose information without consent.

## Changes in effect from 1 December 2025

Blind cord safety anchors: all rental properties must have secured cords on internal window coverings to prevent them forming loops. Rooming house heating standard: fixed heaters must be installed in each resident's room.

## Changes in effect from 31 March 2026

New standard rental application form: prescribed forms must be used for rental applications. Information collection rules: rental providers are limited to requesting information necessary to assess suitability and verify identity and capacity to pay rent. Ban on third party application and payment fees: rental providers and agents may still use third party platforms, but it is an offence for the businesses that run these to charge a renter fees. Rent increase investigation factors: the matters the Director and the Tribunal consider when deciding whether a rent increase is excessive have been expanded.
