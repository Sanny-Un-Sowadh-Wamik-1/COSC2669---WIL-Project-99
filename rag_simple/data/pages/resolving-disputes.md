## Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria

Renters, rental providers and estate agents can use Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria (RDRV), a free alternative dispute resolution service offered by VCAT. RDRV can help resolve disputes about many renting issues, including rent increases, repairs and bonds. If RDRV can't resolve the issue, it will help you apply to the Victorian Civil and Administrative Tribunal (VCAT). RDRV can be contacted by phone, online or in person at any VCAT office. Each application is case managed by a resolution coordinator, and methods include facilitated discussions and mediation.

## Steps to resolving a renting dispute

1. Know your rights and responsibilities. 2. Talk to your agent or rental provider. 3. Contact Consumer Affairs Victoria. If still unresolved, apply to RDRV. Common dispute topics are bond claims, rental agreements, repairs and maintenance, rent increases, notices to vacate and goods left behind. Urgent issues needing immediate contact include illegal evictions and urgent repairs not addressed by the rental provider.

## Talking about and documenting disputes

Explain the problem and the outcome you want, and which obligations you believe are not being met. Record any agreement in writing. If you can't agree, put complaints in writing or, if applicable, issue a breach of duty notice. When someone breaks the rental agreement in a way that is a breach of duty, a formal breach of duty notice can be the first step.

## Going to VCAT

If RDRV cannot resolve the case, it can help you apply to VCAT, which resolves disputes when other steps have not worked. You will need evidence such as letters, emails, phone records, photos, videos or receipts.

## Further help

Financially disadvantaged renters or those experiencing family violence can contact the Tenancy Assistance and Advocacy Program. Renters facing eviction for rent arrears can use Dear Landlord, a free Justice Connect self-help tool. Consumer Affairs Victoria cannot help with disputes between renters; contact the Dispute Settlement Centre of Victoria. Renters aged 55 and over can contact Housing for the Aged Action Group (HAAG).
