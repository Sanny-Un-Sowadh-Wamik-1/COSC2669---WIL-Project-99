## Rental properties must meet the minimum standards

There are 15 categories of minimum standards for rental properties, and all rental properties must meet the standards for each category. Rental providers and agents must make sure a property meets minimum standards at the time they advertise or offer to let the property, and before the renter moves in. There are special rules for minimum standards in rooming houses.

## When minimum standards apply

Rental properties must meet the minimum standards before they are advertised. For existing rental agreements, the minimum standards in Schedule 4 of the Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021 apply if the agreement started on or after 29 March 2021, or started before 29 March 2021 and became a periodic (month to month) agreement on or after 29 March 2021. They don't apply if the agreement became periodic before 29 March 2021. New minimum rental standards for energy efficiency come into effect from 1 March 2027.

## Bathrooms

A bathroom must have a washbasin and a shower or bath, connected to a reasonable supply of hot and cold water. Showers must have a shower head with a 3-star water efficiency rating; if one cannot be installed, a 1- or 2-star shower head is acceptable.

## Electrical safety

The electrical safety standard commenced on 29 March 2023. Rental properties must have modern switchboards, with circuit breakers and electrical safety switches (residual current devices: RCD, RCCB or RCBO) installed. Rental providers are responsible for engaging an electrician to make the property comply. Older properties with fuse boards may need the entire switchboard replaced.

## Heating

All rental properties must have a fixed heater (not portable) in good working order in the main living area. For rental agreements entered into from 29 March 2023, this must be an energy efficient fixed heater: a non-ducted air conditioner or heat pump with a 2 star or above energy rating, a gas space heater with a 2 star or above energy rating, a ducted or hydronic heating system with an outlet in the main living area, or a solid fuel appliance such as a fireplace or wood burning stove. In some apartments it may be unreasonable to install an energy efficient heater (for example because of cost or owners corporation rules); a fixed heater must still be installed and the renter told before signing.

## Kitchen

The property must have a kitchen with a dedicated cooking and food preparation area, a sink in good working order connected to a reasonable supply of hot and cold water, and a stovetop in good working order with two or more burners. If there is an oven, it must be in good working order.

## Laundry and lighting

If there is a laundry, it must be connected to a reasonable supply of hot and cold water. Inside rooms, corridors and hallways must have access to light: natural light during the day (which can be borrowed from an adjoining room) and artificial light at night.

## Locks

External entry doors must have functioning deadlocks (a deadlatch with at least one cylinder), or locks that can be unlocked with a key from the outside but without a key from the inside. Exceptions include doors that cannot be secured with a deadlock, screen doors in the same frame as an external door, and doors behind another security barrier.

## Mould and damp, structural soundness

All rooms must be free from mould and damp caused by or related to the building structure. The property must be structurally sound and weatherproof.

## Toilets and ventilation

The toilet must be in good working order, connected to a sewerage system, approved wastewater system or council-approved system, and be in a separate room (by itself or in a bathroom or combined bathroom-laundry). All habitable rooms, including bathroom, shower, toilet and laundry, must have adequate ventilation meeting the Building Code of Australia.

## Vermin proof bins

Rental providers must supply a rubbish bin and a recycling bin that are vermin proof and meet council collection standards.

## Windows, window coverings and anchors

All external windows that can open must have a functioning latch to secure them against external entry and must be able to be left open or closed. Windows in rooms likely to be used as bedrooms or living areas must have curtains or blinds that can be closed, block light and provide privacy. From 1 December 2025, all corded internal window coverings, such as blinds and curtains, must have an anchor installed to secure the cords and prevent them forming loops.
