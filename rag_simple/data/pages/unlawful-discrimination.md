## Protected characteristics

In Victoria it is against the law to stop somebody renting a property because of certain personal characteristics, including age; carer status, family responsibilities or parental status; disability; employment; gender identity, lawful sexual activity or sexual orientation; marital status; physical features; pregnancy or breastfeeding; profession, trade or occupation (including being a sex worker); race (including colour, nationality, ethnicity and ethnic origin); religious belief or activity; and sex. Sexual harassment is also against the law. A person who believes they have been discriminated against can contact the Victorian Equal Opportunity and Human Rights Commission (VEOHRC) or apply to VCAT for compensation. Rental providers must give applicants a prescribed statement of information on discrimination.

## Children and renting

A rental provider or agent cannot refuse to rent to someone because a child (under 16) will live with them. The only exceptions are if the property is the rental provider's main residence or is unsuitable for children. A person who disagrees that a property is unsuitable can apply to VCAT.

## Renters with disability and assistance dogs

Rental providers must not discriminate against renters who have or acquire a disability, and should not refuse reasonable changes to the property. People with assistance dogs cannot be discriminated against, asked to remove the dog, asked to leave, or charged extra because of the dog.

## Pets and first-time renters

It is not unlawful discrimination to refuse to rent to someone with a pet, although rental providers can't unreasonably refuse a renter's request to keep a pet. Having never rented before is also not a protected characteristic.
