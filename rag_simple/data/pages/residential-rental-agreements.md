## Rental agreements

A rental agreement used to be called a lease or residential tenancy agreement. It is a contract between the renter and rental provider. A rental agreement should include the amount of rent and how it is paid, how rent increases will be calculated, the length and type of agreement, the bond amount, other conditions and rules, and any special terms. The prescribed form must be used for a written rental agreement. Extra terms can be added if they are not prohibited, but terms cannot take away rights and responsibilities provided by the law. There is no cooling-off period for a rental agreement.

## Types of residential rental agreements

There are 3 types: short fixed-term agreements, long fixed-term agreements, and periodic (month to month) agreements. Short fixed-term agreements are usually 6 or 12 months but can be up to 5 years; they can be written or verbal, but written agreements are recommended (Form 1). If a fixed-term agreement ends and the renter stays without signing a further agreement, it automatically turns into a periodic (month to month) agreement, and for agreements of 5 years or less the terms of the original agreement continue to apply.

## Long fixed-term agreements

Long fixed-term agreements are for more than 5 years (Form 2). Different rules include bond top-ups after 5 years when renewed for another 5 or more years, agreeing modifications before signing, and a cap on costs for leaving early of one month's rent for every full year remaining, up to a maximum of 6 months. Long-term agreements must be in writing; if not, the renter can end the agreement at any time by giving the rental provider 28 days' notice.

## Before moving in

Before a renter moves in, the rental provider or agent must give them a copy of the written rental agreement, a copy of the Renters guide, an emergency phone number for urgent repairs, the rental provider or agent's full name, postal address and (if agreed) email address, a set of keys for each renter who signed the agreement, and a condition report (2 copies or 1 electronic).

## Conditions that cannot be in an agreement

Renters cannot be required to take out any form of insurance; pay additional rent or penalties if they break the rules; pay for preparing the agreement; pay rent in advance in a way that costs extra (other than bank fees); use a third-party service provider nominated by the rental provider (other than an embedded network); pay for or organise maintenance of the rental provider's safety equipment; or indemnify the rental provider. The agreement cannot say that rent will be reduced or rebates paid if the renter does not break the rules; that the renter is bound by a contract they did not agree to in writing; that the renter cannot claim compensation if the premises are not available on the start date; that the renter must pay the rental provider's VCAT filing costs or insurance excess; or that the renter must pay a fixed break fee unless the basis for calculating it is set out in the agreement. A prohibited term is not valid and the rental provider may face penalties.
