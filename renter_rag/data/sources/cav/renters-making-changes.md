## Overview

There are rules about the changes renters can make to a property, and they will often need permission from the rental provider. The rules depend on the type of change, the type of rental property and the length of the rental agreement. A rental provider may require that changes are done by a suitably qualified person.

## Changes that can be made without permission

A renter can install the following without permission: non-permanent window film for insulation, reduced heat transfer or privacy; a wireless doorbell; curtains (without throwing out the original curtains); adhesive child safety locks on drawers and doors; pressure mounted child safety gates; a lock on a letterbox.

If the property is not on the Victorian Heritage Register, a renter can also install without permission: picture hooks or screws for wall mounts, shelves or brackets on any surface except exposed brick or concrete walls; wall anchors to secure furniture (except on exposed brick or concrete); LED light bulbs that don't need new fittings; low flow shower heads (keeping the original); blind or cord anchors; removable safety devices such as alarm systems or security cameras that don't affect neighbours' privacy, can be easily removed and are not hardwired; hardware mounted child safety gates (except on exposed brick or concrete).

## Reasonable changes the rental provider cannot unreasonably refuse

For any other change the renter must ask permission, but the rental provider cannot refuse the following without a good reason: picture hooks, wall anchors or hardware mounted child safety gates on exposed brick or concrete walls; draughtproofing in a property without open flued gas heating; a security system installed by a suitably qualified person (with an invoice naming the installer); flyscreens on doors and windows; a vegetable or herb garden; a secure letterbox; painting the premises; modifications to secure external gates; conservation work on a registered heritage place. Nor can they unreasonably refuse changes that don't penetrate or permanently change surfaces or structure, are needed for health and safety, are disability-related modifications recommended by an occupational therapist, give access to phone, internet or TV, are reasonable security measures, are needed for the safety of a renter experiencing family violence or protected by a personal safety intervention order, keep the renter from being too hot or cold, or reduce energy and water bills.

## Reasons a rental provider can refuse changes

It might be reasonable to refuse if heritage protections would be affected, the change would breach other legal requirements, it would significantly change the property, it would require changes to other premises or common areas, it would add maintenance costs if not reversed, reversing it is not reasonably practicable, or the property is about to be sold or vacated and a valid notice to vacate has been given.

## Modification bond

The rental provider may require an extra bond (a modification bond) to cover the cost of undoing changes at the end of the agreement. They cannot ask for it if the extra bond would be less than $500, if it is unreasonable compared with the cost of reversing the changes, if the rental provider does not want the changes reversed, or if the changes are funded by a scheme such as the NDIS where restoration is not required.

## If the rental provider does not agree

The renter should first ask why the request was refused. If the renter thinks the rental provider is refusing reasonable changes, they can apply to VCAT for a ruling.

## Short-term and long-term agreements

In a short-term agreement (5 years or less), apart from the allowed list a renter must not make changes without permission; if they do, the rental provider can give a Notice of breach of duty requiring the renter to restore the property or pay the reasonable cost of restoring it. Even if a rental provider gives permission for changes, they do not have to pay for them. In a long-term agreement, changes can be agreed and written into the agreement before signing. Before the end of the agreement, a renter who has made changes must restore the property (allowing for fair wear and tear) or pay the reasonable cost of restoring it, unless the rental provider agrees otherwise, the agreement says so, or the renter paid a modification bond.

## Safety devices

Renters must not remove, deactivate or interfere with safety devices such as smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms, electrical safety switches or pool barriers, except where reasonable (for example dusting a smoke alarm or silencing it after an accidental activation). Renters must not take the batteries out of a smoke alarm.
