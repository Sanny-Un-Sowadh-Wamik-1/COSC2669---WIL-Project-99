## What it means to break a rental agreement

Breaking a rental agreement means a renter leaves without giving notice, or leaves before the end of a fixed-term rental agreement.

## Reasons a renter can end early without paying costs

A renter can end the agreement early without paying costs, with the following minimum notice: the property did not meet the rental minimum standards when the renter moved in (immediate); a long-term agreement was verbal or not in the prescribed form (28 days); the rental provider refused disability-related modifications (14 days); the renter needs to move into special personal care (14 days); the renter is moving into social housing (14 days); the renter needs temporary crisis accommodation (14 days); the rental provider did not disclose before the agreement that the property was being sold (14 days). Documentary evidence is required for special circumstances. In other situations (for example, after the renter receives a notice to vacate for repairs, demolition, business use, owner or family occupation, sale or public purposes), the renter can leave with 14 days notice and VCAT may decide whether any costs apply.

## Costs for breaking an agreement

Renters must cover reasonable costs the rental provider incurs. Lost rent: VCAT decides the compensation; for fixed-term agreements of more than 5 years, compensation cannot be more than one month's rent for each 12 months remaining, up to a maximum of 6 months' rent. Renters don't owe rent once the property is re-let. Advertising costs: renters may have to pay reasonable re-advertising costs. Re-letting fees: renters usually pay a share based on how much of the agreement was left (for example, with 6 months of a 12-month agreement left, half the fee).

## Costs renters don't have to pay

Renters don't have to pay penalties for breaking the agreement, rent after the property is re-let, advertising fees if the property wasn't advertised, or re-letting fees if the owner let the property directly without an agent. Bond is repaid by agreement or VCAT order.

## Disputing costs and hardship

Renters should negotiate first; if they can't agree, either party can apply to VCAT. Renters facing severe hardship (such as job loss, a medical condition or a family tragedy) may apply to VCAT to end the agreement early, and VCAT must consider hardship when deciding compensation.
