## How to challenge a rent increase

Renters can ask Consumer Affairs Victoria to investigate a rent increase they believe is above the market range, using the free rent assessment service. Renters can't be forced to leave a property because they ask for a rent assessment. Renters should first try to negotiate with their rental provider before asking for an investigation.

All renters can request an assessment, including rooming house residents and caravan and residential park residents. Site tenants can only request a review if their site agreement allows non-fixed rent increases.

## Timeline and process

A request must be made within 30 days of receiving the written notice of the rent increase, using the Request for Rental Assessment form. A case officer will contact the renter and an inspector may visit the property. After the investigation a report is given to both parties.

## Challenging high rent without an increase

An assessment of rent that has not increased is only available in limited circumstances: when services or facilities previously provided have been removed without a matching reduction in rent, or when a rooming house resident's rent changes because room occupancy has increased.

## What gets assessed

An assessment considers rent for comparable properties, the change in the consumer price index (CPI), the state of repair of the property, its location and facilities, when previous increases happened, and any relevant non-compliance with the law.

## Next steps

If the assessment finds the rent is excessive and the rental provider will not negotiate, the renter can apply to Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria within 30 days, and the matter may go on to VCAT, which can make an order setting the rent.
