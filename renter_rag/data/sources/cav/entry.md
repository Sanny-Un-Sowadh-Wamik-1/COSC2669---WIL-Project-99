## Right of entry

Rental providers and their agents have the right to enter a property, but there are rules about the reasons they can enter and when. Renters must allow lawful entry when proper notice is given.

## Reasons for entry and notice required

A rental provider may enter for: a general (routine) inspection – 7 days notice; repairs or to meet legal obligations – 24 hours notice; showing the property to prospective renters, buyers or lenders – 48 hours notice; a valuation – 7 days notice; taking photos or videos for advertising – 7 days notice; a suspected breach of the renter's obligations – 24 hours notice; family violence VCAT proceedings – 24 hours notice. The notice must be in writing and state the reason for entry.

## Entry times

The rental provider or their agent can only enter the property between 8 am and 6 pm on any day except a public holiday.

## When renters can refuse entry

Renters may refuse entry if the visit is not between 8 am and 6 pm or is on a public holiday, no written notice was given, the notice was not properly delivered or did not give enough time, the rental provider wants to show the property to prospective renters outside the last 21 days of the agreement, or a general inspection has already been done in the past 6 months.

## Conduct during entry

The rental provider must not enter in an unreasonable manner or stay longer than necessary for the purpose of the entry, unless the renter agrees. The renter does not need to be present if valid notice was given, and cannot be required to leave.

## General inspections

General inspections are only allowed after the first 3 months of the agreement and no more than once every 6 months.

## Showing the property to prospective renters

The property can be shown to prospective renters when the renter has given a notice of intention to vacate or received a notice to vacate. Open inspections are limited to the last 21 days of the agreement, at most twice a week and one hour per visit unless the renter agrees otherwise.

## Showing the property to prospective buyers or lenders

The rental provider must give at least 14 days notice using the Notice of intention to sell the property form before showing the property to buyers, and must make reasonable efforts to agree inspection times. Inspections are limited to twice a week, one hour per visit, unless the renter agrees. The renter must be compensated for each sales inspection: half a day's rent or $30, whichever is greater.

## Photos and videos for advertising

Renters may object to photos or videos that could identify them or reveal sensitive information, show valuable items, or could identify people at risk of violence. If the renter objects, the rental provider cannot use that content. If the renter asks to review images before use, they cannot be published without the renter's written approval.

## Damage during entry

If something belonging to the renter is damaged or taken while the rental provider or agent is on the property, the renter may apply to VCAT for compensation.
